Why build this

Most civic history
is a plaque you
walk past.

This one has 257 footnotes.

A city's development story usually arrives as a marker on a sidewalk or a paragraph in a strategic plan — a claim with nothing behind it you can check. This is the same story told the way a dataset is told: every number carries the institution, the date and the URL it came from, and says out loud how confident it is.

Data projects fail quietly: a confident number, no provenance, and no way to tell a census table from a guess. So every source object here carries a verification status, and the site prints the running count of each.

A comparison, and the figure that complicates it

Public money
cleared it. Public
money is helping
rebuild it.

1957 – 1974 · Federal
$36.8M
In urban renewal grants approved for Tampa. $28.2M of it was actually disbursed.
HUD Urban Renewal Directory, June 1974
via U. of Richmond DSL
→
2019 · Private
$3B
Announced private investment in Water Street Tampa — against up to $100M in CRA infrastructure reimbursement pledged by the city and county.
Strategic Property Partners, 9 July 2019
Public share: $50M city + $50M county

The tidy version of this story is that public money cleared the ground and private money rebuilt it. The site's own data will not support that: both sides of the ledger have public money in them. Nominal dollars, no averaging, no conversion — and the caveat printed next to the number it qualifies.

Your turn
07 / 07
The whole point of putting it in the open

Fork it.
Do your city.

Tampa is not special here — the structure is. Every city has a clearance decade, a highway that went through somebody, and a waterfront somebody is selling. Swap the data layer and the machine runs on your archive.

1
Fork the repo. Keep index.html and js/app.js as they are.
2
Rewrite js/data.js with your city's eras, events and series — one source object per figure.
3
Run node tools/validate-data.mjs and tools/check-links.mjs. They fail you honestly.
4
Publish on GitHub Pages. Send me the link — I want to read it.
