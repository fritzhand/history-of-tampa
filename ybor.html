About

What This Is, and How to Read It

An open-data history of one square mile north-east of downtown Tampa — why it was founded, what it made, and who made it.

Ybor City is unusual among American neighbourhoods in that someone decided to build it. It was not a settlement that grew outward from a crossroads: it was bought, platted and populated on purpose, for one trade, by people who needed somewhere that trade could work. This study follows that decision and its consequences — with a map, a set of charts, a year scrubber and archival photographs.

It is the companion to Downtown Tampa: A Civic Development Autopsy in the same archive, and shares its architecture and its citation standard. Both are domain forks of The 2026 Iran War: A Geoeconomic Autopsy by Sahasrik Ragani at Ahmedabad University. The site is static — no build step and no backend. Everything it knows lives in one cited data file.

How the evidence is graded

Every figure carries a source object naming the institution, the document, the date and a status. Nothing is presented as fact because it sounds right.

  • Confirmed Traced to a primary institutional source that says exactly this. Hero and footer numbers must be confirmed.
  • Pending Published somewhere credible, not yet verified against the primary record. Treat as provisional.
  • Derived An editorial model, not a measurement. The note names the inputs and the reasoning. Do not cite these as primary figures.

The Source Audit prints the live tally: every institution, how many points rest on it, and the status of each. Where sources disagree — and on Ybor City's factory counts and worker totals they often do — both figures are published and neither is averaged.

What is still missing

Ybor City is a neighbourhood, not a census place, so there is no clean official population series for it; every population figure here says exactly what it counts and which boundary it uses. Factory and employment counts come from trade press and contemporary histories more often than from a government register, and are graded accordingly. Those gaps are named on the page rather than smoothed over.

The study area

The Ybor City Historic District and its approaches: the 7th Avenue spine, the surviving cigar factories and the blocks around them. Ybor City is a neighbourhood rather than a census place, and its boundary differs between the landmark district, the city historic district and the 133.1-acre redevelopment area — so each figure records which one it counts.
