Community Redevelopment Area Taxable Value

The public side of the ledger. A Community Redevelopment Area freezes its tax base at a start year and spends the growth above it inside the district. Downtown's base was $454M; the Channel District's, set in 2004, was $39.9M. What each is worth now is the increment those districts have to spend.

Neighborhood Disruption Index (0–100)

Physical disruption peaks for Central Avenue / The Scrub and Ybor during interstate and urban renewal years. South waterfront disruption in 2020 reflects intentional master-planned rebuild (Water Street), not mid-century clearance trauma — different cause, still high change.

Clearance & Displacement

What Urban Renewal and the Interstates Removed

Between 1957 and 1974 the federal government approved $36.8M in urban renewal grants for Tampa and disbursed $28.2M. Against that: 309.6 acres cleared, 2,223 dwellings demolished, and 1,242 families displaced, 91.7 per cent of them recorded as non-white in a city that was roughly 17 per cent Black. Two interstates took an estimated 3,244 more homes. Figures come from the federal Urban Renewal Project Characteristics quarterlies and HUD's Urban Renewal Directory, with the city's own record where the federal series is silent.

Read the family counts with care. They are the estimated displacements officials reported at the time, not verified relocations, and single adults were never counted. Where sources disagree — Ybor City reports 186 families against 953 demolished dwellings — both figures appear, with the discrepancy named rather than resolved.

About

What This Is, and How to Read It

An open-data autopsy of two hundred years of downtown Tampa — what was built, what it cost, and who paid.

Downtown Tampa was a fort, then a railhead, then a cigar port, then a skyline, then a clearance site, then a waterfront. This project follows that arc from 1824 to 2024 with a map, a set of charts, a year scrubber and forty-two archival photographs — and it shows its working for every number on the page.

It is a domain fork of The 2026 Iran War: A Geoeconomic Autopsy: same architecture, same citation standard, different subject. The site is static — no build step, no backend, no tracking. Everything it knows lives in one cited data file.

How the evidence is graded

Every figure carries a source object naming the institution, the document, the date and a status. Nothing is presented as fact because it sounds right.

  • Confirmed Traced to a primary institutional source that says exactly this. Hero and footer numbers must be confirmed.
  • Pending Published somewhere credible, not yet verified against the primary record. Treat as provisional.
  • Derived An editorial model, not a measurement. The note names the inputs and the reasoning. Do not cite these as primary figures.

The Source Audit prints the live tally: every institution, how many points rest on it, and the status of each. Where sources disagree — Ybor City's displacement counts, for instance — both figures are published and neither is averaged.

What is still missing

The port and cigar series remain editorial indices rather than measured output, and three conceptual indices carry derived methodology notes where real data belongs. Those gaps are named on the page rather than smoothed over, and the research brief for closing them is in the repository.
